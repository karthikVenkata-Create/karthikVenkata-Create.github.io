Costco Equity Valuation (Python)

Built a valuation model for Costco that pulls live market data, runs a DCF and a reverse DCF, and stress-tests the result with 10,000 Monte Carlo simulations. Every run is saved to a SQL database. My call is SELL with a $511 target. The price works only if Costco grows about 10% a year for another two decades, and even if everything goes right, a buyer at $992 earns only about 1.6 points more a year than a Treasury bond. Most analysts disagree, and the write-up explains why.

FP&A Budget Variance Pipeline (Python + Excel)

Analyzed $28.2M of 2024 operating spend across 8 departments and 12 months. The plan looked 99.8% accurate, but only because overspends and underspends cancelled out. Counting every miss, accuracy was 95.7%, or $1.21M of misses, and 7 of 96 department-months broke my materiality rule (over $25K and over 5%). I first built this in Excel, found my own headline didn't hold up, rebuilt the workbook, then turned it into a Python pipeline that reconciles to the workbook to the dollar.

Auto Stock Risk & Return Engine (R + Tableau)

Tested whether optimizing a portfolio of five auto stocks (Tesla, Toyota, Honda, BMW, Geely) beats just splitting the money equally, using 3,932 trading days from 2010 to 2026. Rebalancing every year didn't earn more than buy-and-hold (23.9% vs 29.4% a year), but it cut the worst drop from 71% to 39%. Then I ran a walk-forward test where each year's weights only use the previous three years of data. Max-Sharpe roughly tied equal weight on return per unit of risk while trading twice as much, and min-variance badly lagged. Built in R and visualized in three Tableau dashboards. This started as an Excel project and got rebuilt twice.
